# Storyline Planner

Use this before drafting or revising a contest paper, abstract, conclusion, or
core figure captions. The goal is to turn evidence into a clear modeling story.

## Story Spine

For each subquestion, write one spine before prose:

- object: what physical, social, or operational system is studied;
- contradiction: why a naive method is insufficient;
- observable: what the data directly measure;
- target: what the problem asks us to infer or decide;
- model route: baseline, improved model, and final choice;
- evidence: table/figure that proves the choice;
- limitation: remaining risk or scope boundary.

## B-Problem Story Requirements

- State that the single-beam dominant-frequency method is a baseline.
- Explain why angle correction, dispersion, Fresnel effects, and nonlinear
  fitting are considered for the formal solution.
- For Problem 3, make multi-beam interference a decision point, not an appendix
  afterthought.
- Tie every final thickness number to a table and every model-choice claim to a
  residual, comparison, or sensitivity figure.

## Section Checklist

- The first paragraph names the measured data and target quantity.
- The model paragraph introduces variables before equations.
- The solution paragraph reports solver status or computation route.
- The validation paragraph compares against baseline or alternate physics.
- The figure/table paragraph tells the reader what to learn from the artifact.
- The conclusion uses restrained claims and mentions residual risks.

## Anti-Patterns

- Listing formulas without explaining why the route changed.
- Presenting a polished final number before data audit and validation.
- Calling a model innovative without a concrete improvement.
- Repeating internal workflow labels such as skill, registry, benchmark test,
  script path, or CSV filename in the contest-paper main text.
